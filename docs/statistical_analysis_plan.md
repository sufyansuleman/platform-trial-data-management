# Statistical analysis plan

**Trial.** Synthetic Adaptive Platform Trial in Critical Illness
**SAP version.** 1.0
**Effective date.** 21 August 2026
**Status.** Pre-specified. Committed before any analysis code was written; the git history
of this repository shows that ordering, and the ordering is the point.

> **All data is synthetic.** This plan governs the analysis of data generated by the code
> in this repository. It is an independent educational project with no affiliation to any
> real trial, hospital or research group.

---

## 1. Why this document exists and when it may change

An adaptive trial looks at its own data repeatedly and changes its behaviour in response.
That is its strength and its hazard: every additional look is an additional opportunity to
find a pattern that is not there, and every decision made after seeing the data is a
decision that could have been made differently had the data come out otherwise.

Pre-specification is the only defence. Everything in this document - the outcomes, the
models, the priors, the thresholds, the schedule - is fixed before any of it is applied.
Where a number is stated here it is also stated in `config/`, and the analysis code reads
it from there rather than carrying its own copy.

**Amendments.** This plan may be amended, but only by a dated, versioned change recorded
in the change log at the bottom, and only before the analysis it affects is run. An
amendment made after seeing the result it changes is not an amendment; it is a different
study. Every analysis record stamps the SAP version, the decision-rules version and the
priors version it used, so any result can be traced to the rules in force when it was
produced.

## 2. The analysis runs on frozen cuts, never on live data

Every analysis described here takes a **cut ID** and nothing else. The analysis engine
verifies the cut's SHA-256 manifest before it will proceed and refuses to run on a cut
that fails verification.

Three reasons, set out in full in [data_cut_sop.md](data_cut_sop.md):

1. The live database changes underneath you. Sites edit values continuously. An analysis
   run twice on live data can give two answers and neither is wrong.
2. A committee's decision must remain auditable. "What did they actually see?" must have
   an exact answer years later.
3. Including participants who happen to have complete data, rather than all who have
   reached 30 days, selects on the outcome.

## 3. Analysis populations

### Primary: intention to treat

All participants randomised into the domain whose 30-day window closed on or before the
cut's as-of date, analysed **in the arm they were allocated to**, regardless of what
treatment they actually received, whether they completed it, or whether their data is
complete.

Precisely, a participant-domain record is in the ITT population when:

- it has a randomisation date and time in that domain; **and**
- that randomisation date is on or before `as_of_date - 30 days`.

Records are included **whether or not the endpoint is complete**. A participant with
unknown days enters with an incomplete endpoint and an explicit count of them. Excluding
them would select the population on data quality, which correlates with site, which
correlates with case mix and local practice - see [DEC-017](decisions.md).

Records with **no randomisation date** cannot be anchored to a window and are not
evaluable. They are counted and reported, never silently dropped. In the current synthetic
data this is 99 of 3,058 records, and the number appears in every analysis record.

### Sensitivity: complete case

The same analysis restricted to records with `complete == TRUE`, meaning every day in the
30-day window was observed. Reported alongside the primary analysis, not instead of it.

The comparison is itself a finding. In the current data the ITT mean and the complete-case
mean of the primary outcome differ by **1.6 days**, which is larger than the 1-day
equivalence margin defined below. A plan that reported only one of them would be
concealing something that can change a decision.

## 4. Outcomes

### Primary: days alive without life support at 30 days

The number of days within the 30 days after randomisation into that domain on which the
participant was alive **and** free of all of: invasive mechanical ventilation, vasopressor
or inotropic support, and renal replacement therapy. Death within 30 days scores 0.

Derived by `R/derive/days_alive_without_life_support.R`. Its conventions - the window
boundary, what a partial day means, and what a missing day means - are pinned in
[DEC-012](decisions.md), [DEC-013](decisions.md) and [DEC-006](decisions.md), and are
covered by 40 tests written before the function.

### Secondary

1. **All-cause mortality at 30 days.** Binary, from the outcome form's vital status.
2. **All-cause mortality at 90 days.** Binary. Requires 90 days of follow-up, so a
   participant contributes to this outcome in a later cut than they contribute to the
   primary.
3. **Days alive out of hospital at 30 days.** Days within the window on which the
   participant was alive and not admitted to hospital. Death within 30 days scores 0.

Secondary outcomes are reported with posterior probabilities but **do not trigger stopping
decisions**. Only the primary outcome does. Allowing any of several outcomes to stop a
domain multiplies the opportunities to stop early by chance, which is the error adaptive
designs are most prone to.

## 5. Priors

All values in `config/priors.yml`.

### The governing distinction

**A prior on the baseline event rate may use existing evidence. A prior on the treatment
effect may not.**

These are different kinds of quantity and deserve different treatment. How sick this
population is, and how often patients like these die, is known: it has been measured in
cohorts and in the control arms of previous trials, and pretending otherwise throws away
real information for no benefit. A Beta(2, 3) prior on 30-day mortality, centred at 40%
and worth about five observations, encodes what is already established and is swamped by
the data within the first few dozen participants.

The treatment effect is different in kind. The trial exists **because nobody knows the
answer**. A prior centred anywhere other than zero encodes a belief the trial was
commissioned to test, and it does so invisibly: the posterior will be shifted toward that
belief, and a reader seeing only the posterior cannot tell how much of it came from the
data. The result would partly be a restatement of what the person who wrote the prior
already thought.

The treatment-effect prior is therefore centred on **no difference** and kept wide. It is
symmetric - no more willing to find benefit than harm - because an asymmetric prior on a
treatment effect makes the trial more likely to reach one conclusion than the other before
a single participant is enrolled.

### Primary outcome

Each arm's mean is given a Normal(15, 10²) prior. The centre reflects published critical
care cohorts; the standard deviation of 10 days on an outcome bounded at 0 to 30 is wide
enough that roughly forty participants per arm dominate it.

The implied prior on the difference between arms is Normal(0, 14.14²), centred on zero by
construction. It is stated explicitly in the config so its width can be read rather than
derived.

The residual standard deviation is estimated from the pooled data in the cut and held
fixed, rather than given its own prior and integrated over. This is a deliberate
simplification: it makes the posterior conjugate and therefore closed-form and exactly
reproducible. At the sample sizes involved - hundreds per arm - the uncertainty in the
standard deviation contributes very little to the uncertainty in the difference of means,
which is what the decision rules read. The prior sensitivity analysis in section 8 is
where the cost of this assumption becomes visible if it matters.

### Mortality

Beta(2, 3) per arm: mean 0.4, worth about five prior observations. The posterior is
Beta(2 + deaths, 3 + survivors) - exact, deterministic, and explainable in a single line
to a committee.

## 6. Models

| Outcome | Model | Why this and not something more elaborate |
|---|---|---|
| Days alive without life support | Normal-normal conjugate on the arm means | The decision rules read a **difference in means**. The outcome is bounded and heavily zero-inflated by mortality, so it is not normal; but the difference of two means over hundreds of observations is well behaved regardless, and a closed-form posterior is exactly reproducible in a way that a sampler is not. |
| Mortality at 30 and 90 days | Beta-binomial conjugate | Exact. The posterior is one line. |
| Days alive out of hospital | Normal-normal conjugate | As the primary. |

No MCMC is used anywhere. The reasoning is in [DEC-009](decisions.md), and the decisive
argument is reproducibility: the repository asserts that a historical analysis regenerates
**bit-identically**, and that is a property of the arithmetic in a conjugate model but only
a conditional property of a sampler, holding for a fixed seed, chain count, compiler and
library version. The strongest claim the project makes should not depend on a toolchain
being byte-stable across machines and years.

**What this costs.** Covariate adjustment is limited to stratified analysis rather than a
fitted regression, and there is no partial pooling across sites. If a future amendment
requires an adjusted continuous model with site-level random effects, this decision must be
revisited and `rstanarm` is the fallback. That trigger is recorded so the choice is
revisited deliberately rather than defended out of habit.

Quantities without a closed form - chiefly P(|difference| < margin) - are computed from
100,000 draws from the closed-form posteriors, with the seed fixed in `config/priors.yml`
so the number is identical on every run.

## 7. Decision rules

All values in `config/decision_rules.yml`. Applied per domain at every adaptive analysis,
on the **primary outcome only**.

| Decision | Condition |
|---|---|
| `stop_superiority` | P(arm better than comparator) > **0.99** |
| `stop_inferiority` | P(arm better than comparator) < **0.01** |
| `stop_equivalence` | P(\|mean difference\| < 1 day) > **0.90** |
| `continue` | none of the above |

### Why these numbers

**0.99 for superiority** is deliberately stringent. A platform trial performs many analyses
across several domains, and each is an opportunity to stop early on a chance excursion. The
threshold is calibrated by simulation under a null scenario (section 9) rather than
asserted, and the calibration is reported in the operating characteristics document.

**0.01 for inferiority**, stated separately rather than as `1 - superiority`, because the
two are not mirror images. The cost of continuing to randomise patients into a harmful arm
is not the same as the cost of continuing a beneficial one, so the thresholds must be able
to move independently even if they currently happen to be symmetric.

**A 1-day equivalence margin.** A difference smaller than one day alive without life
support in 30 is not a difference a clinician would change practice for, and not one a
patient would notice. Declaring practical equivalence at that margin says the arms are
close enough that continuing to randomise is not a good use of patients.

That margin is also why the analysis reports ITT and complete-case results side by side.
The two differ by 1.6 days in the current data - **larger than the margin itself** - which
means the handling of missing days could by itself determine whether a domain is declared
equivalent. That is stated here, in advance, rather than discovered afterwards.

## 8. Adaptation

### Analysis schedule

The first adaptive analysis in a domain occurs when **1,000 participants** in that domain
have completed 30-day follow-up, and thereafter every **250** completed participants, to a
maximum of **10,000**.

### Response-adaptive randomisation

Begins **after the first adaptive analysis** - allocation stays fixed until there is
evidence to adapt to. Allocation is then set proportional to each arm's posterior
probability of being best, subject to a floor of **40%** per arm, with the remainder
renormalised.

The floor is the important part. Without it, an early run of chance can starve an arm of
participants before the evidence is real, and a trial that has stopped recruiting to an arm
cannot recover the information it needs to find out it was wrong.

### Application to the current synthetic dataset

The schedule above is the design. The synthetic trial in this repository is smaller than
the design contemplates: 3,058 participant-domain records in total, of which FLUID has
1,373, ANTICOAG 890 and BUFFER 795. Only **FLUID** reaches the 1,000-completed threshold,
and only once.

This is stated plainly rather than quietly rescaling the thresholds to make more analyses
happen. The design's thresholds are the pre-specified ones; the demonstration exercises
them at the scale available. Where the operating characteristics report needs many
analyses to be informative, it simulates trials at the design's scale rather than reusing
the small synthetic dataset.

## 9. Sensitivity analyses

### Prior sensitivity

Every decision is recomputed under three prior sets, defined in `config/priors.yml`:

| Set | Treatment-effect prior SD | Asks |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-specified | 10 days | The planned analysis |
| Sceptical | 2 days | Does the conclusion survive a reader who does not believe the treatment works? |
| Vague | 100 days | Does the conclusion depend on the prior at all? |

**The headline result is whether the decision changes, not what each posterior looks
like.** A table of three near-identical posteriors is reassuring and uninformative; a
domain that stops for superiority under the pre-specified prior and continues under the
sceptical one is the finding, and it must be reported as one.

### Prior predictive checks

Before any data is analysed, datasets are simulated from the priors alone and compared
against the range of clinically plausible trial results. A prior that implies trials which
could not happen - mean days alive without life support outside 0 to 30, or mortality near
0% or 100% - is wrong, however weak it looks in a table. The check and its plot are
included in the analysis report.

### Complete case

As section 3.

## 10. Missing outcome data

Stated explicitly, because leaving it implicit is how missing data quietly becomes an
assumption.

| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Some days in the window unobserved | The participant is **in** the ITT analysis with the observed days counted and the unobserved days counted as not free of support. `unknown_days` is reported. This understates the endpoint rather than inflating it - see [DEC-006](decisions.md). |
| No randomisation date | **Not evaluable.** Excluded from the analysis and reported as a count in every analysis record. |
| Vital status unknown at 30 days | Treated as alive for the primary outcome, and reported separately. The count must be small; if it is not, the analysis is unreliable and the report says so. |
| Participant still within their window | Not in the cut at all. Not a missing-data problem. |

No imputation is performed for the primary analysis. Imputing unobserved days would
manufacture the very observations the endpoint counts, and would do so most often at the
sites whose data is worst - precisely where the manufactured values would be least
trustworthy.

## 11. What is reported

Every adaptive analysis produces a signed analysis record containing the cut ID and its
manifest hash, the SAP version, the decision-rules version, the priors version, package
versions, the git commit, a timestamp, and all posterior quantities and decisions. The
record is written before the report is rendered, and the report reads it. That ordering
means the report cannot show a number the record does not contain.

For each domain and arm:

- Posterior mean difference with a 95% credible interval
- P(superiority), P(inferiority), P(practical equivalence)
- The decision under the pre-specified rules
- Updated allocation probabilities under RAR
- The same, under each sensitivity prior
- ITT and complete-case results side by side
- Counts: randomised, completed follow-up, not evaluable, incomplete
